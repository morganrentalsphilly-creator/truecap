# Prompt for Claude Cowork: benchmark the best UI-first sites, run an adversarial audit of usetruecap.com, and write the Claude Code prompt that fixes what you find

You are a design director and a hostile QA lead at once. You have three jobs, in order, and the third depends on the first two. Work from evidence you gather yourself in a browser; never from memory of what these sites "usually" look like.

## Context you need before you start

**The product.** TrueCap (https://usetruecap.com) is a rental-property analyzer. A real estate agent with investor clients (the primary user, on a phone at a showing) or a buy-and-hold investor (arriving from an ad) pastes a listing and gets a verdict: whether the deal meets a Buy Box, the Offer Ceiling (the highest price that still meets it), cash flow, DSCR, cap rate, cash-on-cash, a Deal score, and a memo the client can open without an account. Free runs the analysis; Pro ($29.99/mo or $300/yr) and Agent Pro add saved deals, co-branding, compare, PDF and the client roster. The repo is the truecap repo (Next.js 16, React 19, Tailwind v4, Radix, Motion for React, NumberFlow).

**What just shipped (2026-10-21).** A full UI overhaul to a system called Blueprint (a drafting sheet with a faint cobalt grid, graphite ink, one cobalt for actions, one warm highlight stroke under the Offer Ceiling, Hanken Grotesk with tabular figures, Geist Mono for metadata), then a polish pass. The homepage hero is a live instrument: drag the price and every figure rolls; the result page's price track drags too as a what-if; the report under the decision card is on the system. Read, in this order: `DESIGN.md` (the system and its decisions log), `PRODUCT.md` (audience and copy guardrails), `CLAUDE.md` §1, §8 and §9 (what Claude may not touch and the design workflow), `OVERHAUL.md` (the founder's brief), `docs/overhaul-2026-10/polish/README.md` (the last critique, what changed, what was left and why), `docs/overhaul-2026-10/phase-8/README.md` (the measurements and the QA tools).

**Known open items.** Do not re-discover these as if new; confirm or refute them with evidence and rank them against what you find: the signed-in home opens with four KPI tiles instead of the deal list with a gap-to-asking column; the scroll story crossfades three cards instead of one sheet that accumulates; `/analyze?sample=1` shows the form for about 350 ms before hydration; blocking time rose to 152 ms on `/` and 181 ms on `/analyze` with the new islands (budget 200 ms); Safari has never been exercised by automation; "$49.17/mo" for Agent Pro annual is a pricing-page presentation the founder decides; the phone homepage puts eight text blocks before the instrument because the copy is pinned.

## Part 1: benchmark the most interactive, intuitive, UI-first websites

1. Pick eight to ten sites that are widely regarded as the best interactive, intuitive, UI-first product sites today, weighted toward fintech, developer tools and productivity (candidates to verify, not a list to accept: Stripe, Linear, Vercel, Mercury, Ramp, Raycast, Loom, Framer, Family, Arc, Pitch, Lemon Squeezy). Visit each in a real browser at 1440 and 390 wide. Reject any that are just pretty; keep the ones where the interaction teaches the product.
2. For each site record, with screenshots: the first five seconds (what moves, what is still, what the visitor can touch first); the one memorable moment and how it is built (scroll choreography, direct manipulation, a live demo, a figure that rolls); how pricing is presented and how the toggle, the cards and the comparison table behave; how a result or a dashboard carries density without clutter; the micro-interactions on buttons, cards, inputs and handles (press, hover, focus, release) with their timings; empty, loading and error states; how they behave with reduced motion and with JavaScript off; how fast they are (Lighthouse mobile).
3. Distil a pattern library of at most twenty patterns, each with: the site that does it best, why it works for the visitor, the cost (performance, accessibility, complexity), and a verdict for TrueCap: fits the Blueprint system and the product truth, fits with a change, or does not fit (and why; "it is not a deal analyzer" is a valid reason).

## Part 2: an adversarial audit of usetruecap.com

Assume the site is worse than it looks and go looking for proof. Use the live site, not the repo, for behaviour; use the repo only to explain what you found.

1. Walk every public flow as four people, with screenshots and timings: an agent at a showing on an iPhone over slow 4G (the real Safari if you have it; otherwise say so); an investor from an ad on Android Chrome who wants the answer in ten seconds; a sceptic who doubts every number and looks for the source, the method and the catch; a screen-reader and keyboard-only user. Flows: the homepage top to bottom with every interaction; the sample deal; a real listing pasted into the analyzer (use a public Zillow or Redfin URL, then an address, then a bare price and rent); the result and everything under it; the memo and the share page; pricing to the start of checkout; sign up; the 404.
2. Break it on purpose: a cash purchase (no loan), zero rent, negative cash flow, a $20,000 and a $5,000,000 price, a very long address, emoji, paste from Excel, back and forward mid-flow, refresh mid-run, two tabs, 200% zoom, a 320px viewport, reduced motion, JavaScript off, a blocked third-party domain, a slow connection where the hero's scripts arrive after the visitor has started dragging.
3. Judge against the benchmark bar from Part 1, not against "fine": where does TrueCap feel dead, generic, timid, slow, cluttered, or like a template; where is the one memorable moment missing on a page; where does the motion feel arbitrary or absent; where does the copy explain instead of show; where would the sceptic leave.
4. Run the project's own instruments and report their numbers next to yours: `.claude/skills/impeccable/scripts/impeccable detect --json --viewport 1440x900 <url>` and `390x844`; the Lighthouse config in `docs/overhaul-2026-10/tools/lhci-running-server.json` (against a local production build: the recipe is in `docs/overhaul-2026-10/brief.md` §2 and `CLAUDE.md` §9); `npm run perf:homepage`; the QA scripts in `docs/overhaul-2026-10/tools/` (keyboard, reduced motion, no-JS, slow 4G, engines).
5. Write the audit as a findings table: id, surface, severity P0 (blocks the task or breaks trust) to P3 (polish), the evidence (screenshot path, measurement, DOM fact), what the best site in Part 1 does instead, and the fix in one sentence. Then the five biggest opportunities in prose, each tied to a benchmark pattern. Be direct: name the element, say what is wrong and why it matters to the agent at the showing. No "consider exploring".
